## 1. Executive Summary
This research review synthesizes foundational and peer-reviewed literature across IEEE, ACM, Springer, Elsevier, and NIST publications regarding Deep Learning-based Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS). The goal is to establish empirical justification for the architectural decisions within **Guardian-Sentinel**:
1. Why Multilayer Perceptrons (MLP) outperform classical models on high-dimensional flow attributes.
2. The role of 1D-CNNs and the risks of assuming spatial adjacency in tabular flow features.
3. Why unsupervised Deep Autoencoders are essential for capturing Zero-Day exploits.
4. The mathematical necessity of hybrid supervised/unsupervised consensus arbiters.
5. Techniques for handling severe class imbalance (SMOTE-Tomek, Cost-Sensitive Weights, Focal Loss).

## 3. Key Theoretical Takeaways & Design Decisions

### A. Deep MLP as the Supervised Core
Tabular network flows do not possess native translation invariance (like images) or strict sequential syntax (like natural language). Therefore, fully connected Multi-Layer Perceptrons with **Batch Normalization** and **Dropout** provide the optimal inductive bias for mapping non-linear interactions across high-dimensional flow statistics without artificial sequential inductive assumptions.

### B. Unsupervised Reconstruction as the Zero-Day Shield
Supervised neural networks assign high probabilities to known classes based on closed-world assumptions. A zero-day exploit with completely novel payload structures has no representation in the output softmax layer. Training an Autoencoder exclusively on **Benign traffic** creates a learned manifold of legitimate behavior; any out-of-distribution attack triggers elevated Mean Squared Error (MSE), exposing the exploit before a formal signature exists.

### C. The Danger of Arbitrary Tomek Links on 2.8M Rows
Applying Tomek Links pairwise distance checks ($\mathcal{O}(N^2)$) on a machine with 8 GB RAM will lead to immediate `MemoryError` and page-file thrashing. The literature dictates performing resampling on **controlled, stratified training partitions**, and comparing it rigorously against **cost-sensitive Focal Loss**, which operates in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ space per batch.
